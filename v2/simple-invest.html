About

The challenge

A product built for Singapore assumes Singapore: its currency, its regulations, its support model. Expanding Simple Invest to Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia meant none of those held. The risk was either rebuilding the product four times, or shipping a Singapore product that quietly broke in three markets.

The project

Bring UOB's self-serve investing to three new Southeast Asian markets on one system. Adapt what each market's rules, currency, and habits demanded, while keeping the core experience, and the codebase, shared.

Process
01

Exploration & discovery

Audited the Singapore product to separate market-agnostic elements from Singapore-specific ones, and mapped regulatory constraints per country with regional teams.

02

Localization & iteration

Designed localized variants for each market: language handling, layout density, risk-disclosure flows, and product presentation, all on a shared component base.

03

Cross-regional collaboration

Coordinated across four countries with product, engineering, and legal, navigating different release timelines and compliance reviews while holding the Singapore baseline.

Key features

Self-serve investment capabilities

Problem

Indonesia, Thailand, and Malaysia lacked practical in-branch support. Call-center reliance created delays and strained service.

Solution

Indonesia and Thailand used chatbot guidance. Malaysia leaned on detailed FAQs and tailored error messages, so customers could manage investments independently.

Localized UI experiences

Problem

Indonesian currency values reach 14 to 16 digits, overcrowding screens built around clean Singapore-dollar amounts.

Solution

Dynamic number formatting abbreviated large values, surfacing B for billions, so the hierarchy stayed readable at any digit count.

Outcome
Localization is not translation. It is layout, hierarchy, and support model, decided market by market on a shared base.

Simple Invest reached Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia on one component system rather than four codebases. Shared foundations let the markets develop in parallel without bottlenecking each other, held the experience consistent across four variants, and left reusable localization patterns behind.

The clearest lesson was to align legal and regulatory constraints early. They reshape layout, not just copy, so finding them late means redesigning, not re-labelling.
